## 1. Introduction

**What is DPDP?**

DPDP stands for Digital Personal Data Protection.

The Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023 is an Indian law introduced to protect individuals' digital personal data and regulate how organisations collect, use, store, process and share such data.

It establishes a framework that balances:

- **Individual Privacy:** Protecting the personal data and privacy of individuals.
- **Lawful Processing:** Ensuring organisations process personal data for lawful purposes.
- **Accountability:** Making organisations responsible for protecting personal data.
- **Individual Rights:** Giving individuals control over their personal data.

**Why is DPDP important?**

- Protects individuals' digital personal information.
- Promotes transparency in data processing.
- Ensures consent-based processing.
- Reduces the risk of personal data misuse.
- Establishes accountability for organisations.
- Provides rights and remedies to individuals.

## 2. History of DPDP in India

The development of India's data protection framework:

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| 2017 | Supreme Court recognised the Right to Privacy as a Fundamental Right in the Justice K.S. Puttaswamy judgment. |
| 2017 | Government established the Justice B.N. Srikrishna Committee to recommend a data protection framework. |
| 2018 | Justice B.N. Srikrishna Committee submitted its report and draft Personal Data Protection Bill. |
| 2019 | Personal Data Protection Bill, 2019 was introduced in Parliament. |
| 2022 | Personal Data Protection Bill, 2019 was withdrawn. A new draft framework was subsequently released for consultation. |
| 2023 | Digital Personal Data Protection Bill was introduced and passed by Parliament. |
| 11 August 2023 | DPDP Act, 2023 received Presidential Assent. |
| 3 January 2025 | Draft DPDP Rules, 2025 were published for public consultation. |
| 13 November 2025 | Final DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified in the Official Gazette. |
| 13 November 2025 | Phased commencement of the DPDP Act began. |

## 3. DPDP Act, 2023

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023) establishes the legal framework for protecting digital personal data in India.

**Overview:**

- Total Sections: 44
- Total Chapters: 9
- Main Focus: Personal Data Protection, Consent, Individual Rights and Organisational Accountability.
- Applicable Authority: Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY).

## 4. DPDP Rules, 2025

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified by MeitY on 13 November 2025.

The Rules provide the operational requirements and procedures necessary to implement the provisions of the DPDP Act, 2023.

**Overview:**
- Total Rules: 23
- Total Schedules: 7
- Notified: 13 November 2025
- Purpose: To specify how the requirements of the Act must be implemented.

### All 23 Rules – Short Explanation

| Rule | Topic | What needs to be implemented / Expected outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short Title and Commencement | Defines the name of the Rules and their phased commencement dates. |
| 2 | Definitions | Defines terms used in the Rules, including user account and verifiable consent. |
| 3 | Notice Given by Data Fiduciary | Provide a clear, standalone notice explaining personal data collected, processing purposes and ways to withdraw consent or exercise rights. |
| 4 | Registration and Obligations of Consent Manager | Establish registration requirements and operational responsibilities for Consent Managers. |
| 5 | Processing of Personal Data for Government Benefits and Services | Ensure government-related processing follows the standards specified in the Second Schedule. |
| 6 | Reasonable Security Safeguards | Implement encryption, masking, access controls, logging, monitoring, backups and other security measures. |
| 7 | Intimation of Personal Data Breach | Notify affected individuals without delay and provide the required breach information to the Board. |
| 8 | Time Period for Specified Purpose to Be Deemed as No Longer Being Served | Define retention and erasure timelines for specified Data Fiduciaries and purposes. |
| 9 | Contact Information for Processing-Related Questions | Publish contact details of the DPO, where applicable, or another authorised contact person. |
| 10 | Verifiable Consent for Processing Personal Data of Children | Obtain verifiable parental consent before processing children's personal data, subject to applicable provisions and exemptions. |
| 11 | Verifiable Consent for Persons with Disabilities Having Lawful Guardians | Verify the legal authority of the guardian before obtaining consent. |
| 12 | Exemptions from Certain Obligations for Children's Data | Specify eligible classes and purposes exempt from certain child-data obligations under Section 9. |
| 13 | Additional Obligations of Significant Data Fiduciary | Conduct periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments, audits and algorithmic due diligence, with specified data localisation measures where applicable. |
| 14 | Rights of Data Principals | Publish methods for exercising rights, identity requirements and grievance redressal procedures. |
| 15 | Transfer of Personal Data Outside India | Follow restrictions and requirements specified by the Central Government for certain foreign disclosures. |
| 16 | Exemption for Research, Archiving or Statistical Purposes | Follow the specified standards when processing data for eligible research, archiving or statistical purposes. |
| 17 | Appointment of Chairperson and Other Members | Establish the selection and appointment procedure for Board Members. |
| 18 | Salary, Allowances and Service Conditions of Chairperson and Members | Define the service benefits and conditions of Board Members. |
| 19 | Procedure for Board Meetings and Authentication of Orders | Establish meeting procedures, decision-making and authentication of official orders. |
| 20 | Functioning of Board as Digital Office | Enable digital handling of Board proceedings and related activities. |
| 21 | Appointment and Service Conditions of Board Employees | Define appointment and service conditions of Board officers and employees. |
| 22 | Appeal to Appellate Tribunal | Specify the digital appeal procedure and applicable fee requirements. |
| 23 | Calling for Information from Data Fiduciary or Intermediary | Enable the Central Government to request specified information from Data Fiduciaries or intermediaries. |

## 5. Implementation Timeline

The DPDP Act and Rules follow a phased implementation approach.

### DPDP Act, 2023

| Effective Date | Provisions |
|---|---|
| 13 November 2025 | Section 2, Sections 18–26, 35, 38–43 and Section 44(1) and (3), along with Section 1(2). |
| 13 November 2026 | Section 6(9) and Section 27(1)(d). |
| 13 May 2027 | Sections 3–17, Section 6(1)–(8) and (10), Section 27 except clause (d) of subsection (1), Sections 28–34, 36–37 and Section 44(2). |

### DPDP Rules, 2025

| Effective Date | Rules | Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| 13 November 2025 | Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 | Commencement, definitions and Data Protection Board administration. |
| 13 November 2026 | Rule 4 | Registration and obligations of Consent Managers. |
| 13 May 2027 | Rules 3, 5–16 and 22–23 | Operational requirements covering notices, security, breach reporting, consent, rights, retention and related matters. |

**Important:** As of November 2026, Rule 4 and the corresponding provisions of the Act have commenced. The provisions scheduled for May 2027 are yet to commence.

## 6. Important Terminologies

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Personal Data | Any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to that data. |
| Digital Personal Data | Personal data available in digital form. |
| Data Principal | The individual to whom the personal data relates. |
| Data Fiduciary | An individual or organisation that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data. |
| Data Processor | An individual or organisation that processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary. |
| Consent | A free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous agreement to process personal data. |
| Consent Manager | A registered entity that enables individuals to give, manage, review and withdraw consent. |
| Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF) | A Data Fiduciary notified by the Central Government based on specified factors and risk assessments. |
| Personal Data Breach | Unauthorised processing or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction or loss of access to personal data that compromises its confidentiality, integrity or availability. |
| Data Protection Officer (DPO) | An individual appointed by a Significant Data Fiduciary under Section 10 of the Act. |

## 7. Key Roles and Authorities

### 1. Data Principal
- The individual whose personal data is being processed.
- Has rights over their personal data.
- Can give or withdraw consent.
- Can request access, correction and erasure.
- Can raise grievances.

### 2. Data Fiduciary
- Determines why and how personal data is processed.
- Obtains consent or relies on another permitted lawful ground.
- Provides privacy notices.
- Implements security safeguards.
- Ensures compliance with the Act and Rules.
- Remains responsible for processing carried out on its behalf by Data Processors.

### 3. Data Processor
- Processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary.
- Works according to the Data Fiduciary's instructions and contractual arrangements.
- Must follow applicable security and data protection requirements.

### 4. Data Protection Officer (DPO)
- Appointed by a Significant Data Fiduciary.
- Acts as the point of contact for grievance redressal.
- Represents the Significant Data Fiduciary under the Act.
- Must be based in India.
- Is responsible to the Board of Directors or similar governing body.

**Important:** The Act specifically requires a DPO for Significant Data Fiduciaries. Other Data Fiduciaries must publish the contact information of a DPO, if applicable, or another person who can answer questions about personal data processing.

### 5. Consent Manager
- Registered with the Data Protection Board.
- Provides a platform to manage consent.
- Enables individuals to give, review and withdraw consent.
- Maintains records of consent and related notices.

### 6. Data Protection Board of India (DPBI)
- Established under Section 18 of the DPDP Act.
- Handles complaints and inquiries.
- Investigates personal data breaches and non-compliance.
- Issues directions and imposes monetary penalties as permitted by the Act.

### 7. Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)
- The central ministry responsible for administering the DPDP framework.
- Notifies rules and related requirements.
- Exercises the powers assigned to the Central Government under the Act.

## 8. Rights and Duties of Data Principals

### Rights of Data Principals

| Right | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Right to Access Information | Obtain information about personal data being processed and relevant sharing activities. |
| Right to Correction | Correct inaccurate or misleading personal data. |
| Right to Completion | Complete incomplete personal data. |
| Right to Updating | Update personal data. |
| Right to Erasure | Request deletion of personal data, subject to applicable retention requirements. |
| Right to Grievance Redressal | Raise complaints regarding data processing and exercise of rights. |
| Right to Nominate | Nominate another individual to exercise rights in case of death or incapacity. |

### Duties of Data Principals

- Follow applicable laws while exercising rights.
- Do not impersonate another person.
- Do not suppress material information.
- Do not file false or frivolous complaints.
- Provide authentic information when requesting correction or erasure.

## 9. Responsibilities of Data Fiduciaries

Major responsibilities under the DPDP framework include:

1. Process personal data only for lawful purposes.
2. Provide clear privacy notices.
3. Obtain valid consent wherever consent is the applicable ground.
4. Allow individuals to withdraw consent.
5. Maintain accuracy and completeness of personal data where required.
6. Implement reasonable security safeguards.
7. Report personal data breaches as prescribed.
8. Ensure appropriate contracts with Data Processors.
9. Delete personal data when the purpose is no longer served, subject to legal retention requirements.
10. Establish grievance redressal mechanisms.
11. Provide contact information for processing-related questions.
12. Ensure additional compliance requirements where classified as a Significant Data Fiduciary.

## 10. DPDP Compliance Requirements

The following are important areas organisations should consider when preparing for DPDP compliance:

| Area | Key Requirement |
|---|---|
| Data Inventory | Identify personal data collected, stored, processed and shared. |
| Data Mapping | Understand data flows and processing activities. |
| Privacy Notice | Prepare clear and understandable privacy notices. |
| Consent Management | Obtain, record and manage valid consent where applicable. |
| Data Principal Rights | Establish processes for access, correction, erasure and grievances. |
| Data Processor Management | Maintain appropriate contractual arrangements and oversight. |
| Access Control | Restrict personal data access to authorised individuals. |
| Data Security | Implement encryption, masking and other reasonable safeguards. |
| Logging and Monitoring | Maintain appropriate logs and monitor access to personal data. |
| Breach Management | Establish detection, response and notification procedures. |
| Data Retention | Define retention periods and secure deletion procedures. |
| Children's Data | Implement applicable age and parental consent verification requirements. |
| DPO and Contact Point | Publish the required contact information. |
| Significant Data Fiduciary | Conduct DPIAs, audits and other additional requirements where applicable. |
| Cross-Border Transfers | Review applicable government restrictions and requirements. |

## 11. Key Takeaways

- DPDP is India's legal framework for protecting digital personal data.
- The DPDP Act, 2023 contains 44 Sections across 9 Chapters.
- The DPDP Rules, 2025 contain 23 Rules and 7 Schedules.
- Data Principals have rights over their personal data.
- Data Fiduciaries are responsible for lawful processing and data protection.
- Data Processors handle personal data on behalf of Data Fiduciaries.
- Significant Data Fiduciaries have additional compliance responsibilities, including appointing a DPO.
- The Data Protection Board of India handles complaints, inquiries and enforcement.
- Organisations must prepare for the applicable requirements according to the phased commencement timeline.
